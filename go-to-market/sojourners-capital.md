# Sojourner's Capital

**A chartered firm that plans and project-manages UK exits, and the founder is already their client** — they handled his own departure tax work. Profile: [Sojourner's Capital — firm profile](../Inbox/sojourners-capital-profile.md).

This is the strongest asset in the go-to-market, and it exists already.

## Why they fit

They are the top entry on the [partner list](./partners-and-distribution.md), and four things raise them well above it.

**They project-manage UK exits.** They own the departure checklist. Everything a client sorts out before leaving passes through them, and medical history is a line item nobody puts on it.

**They already run vetted panels.** Property investment is delivered "with vetted partners", and the firm itself is described as working through a vetted panel. The mechanism exists — the question is admission, not invention.

**They publish.** A blog, webinar recordings, free guides, a relocation ebook, a European tax directory. **They have an audience and a content machine, and both are pointed at exactly our buyer.**

**Their clients are our target, precisely.** Their copy says "high-net-worth families", but observed engagements ran at **£1,000 and £1,500**, with average spend estimated under £5,000 *(owner, stated 2026-08-20)*. That is mid-market professional — the [financially comfortable British leaver](../market/target-customer.md) the business already targets, not the [Medical Family Office](../market/competitors/sip-medical-family-office.md) tier.

## The ask is content, not referrals

The conventional partner approach is to request introductions. **Do the opposite: offer them something for their audience.**

- A guest piece — *what happens to your medical history when you leave the UK*
- A chapter or appendix for the **strategic relocation ebook**
- A **webinar** slot, which is the format they already run
- A medical-records line on the exit checklist itself

Why this is the right move here:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **It is a gift, not a request** | Nothing is being asked of the relationship, so nothing is spent — the constraint that keeps [other advisers](first-customers.md) waiting does not apply |
| **It borrows an audience** | Far faster than building one, and the audience is pre-qualified by having hired an exit-planning firm |
| **It plays to the strength** | Writing authoritatively about records is the strongest available skill; asking for referrals is the weakest — see [publishing](publishing.md) |
| **It creates the referral conversation later** | A firm that has published your work has already vouched for you once |

## The story it opens with

They were present for the exact event the company exists because of:

> You handled the financial side of my exit. What happened to my medical records during the same move is why I started this.

No cold approach can open that way.

## What only they can answer

Worth a conversation regardless of what comes of the content offer:

1. When handling departures, **does medical history ever come up?** Unprompted, or never?
2. What is on the **exit checklist**, and who decides what goes on it?
3. **How does something get onto the vetted panel** — what is required, what disqualifies?
4. What do clients ask about that **you have nowhere to send them**?
5. What has gone wrong before when you referred someone out?

Question 4 is the one worth travelling for. A firm that project-manages exits accumulates a list of unmet client needs, and it is the closest thing to a free market survey available.

**Do not ask for referrals in this conversation.** There is nothing to show yet, and an early ask converts a warm relationship into a soft no that is hard to reopen.

## What their client base implies

**This channel sells the entry product**, at £795–£995 from [pricing](../commercial/pricing.md). A £3,000 offer would be out of proportion to everything else in their client's exit budget.

**The price anchor is unusually favourable.** Their client has just paid £1,000–£1,500 to have the financial side of the move handled properly. A comparable fee to have the medical side handled lands in the same mental category — *things I pay for to leave the UK properly* — rather than being weighed against a £399 records pack.

> The reference price is not the competitor. It is the adjacent service they have already agreed to buy.

That is the strongest argument available for the [missing middle](../market/missing-middle.md), and the first evidence for it from the demand side.

**A firm at £1,000 an engagement runs on volume**, which means more clients passing through than a true private-client practice would have — better for a referral relationship, not worse.

**Their geography is European**, not Gulf — golden visas and a European tax directory. [The launch wedge](./launch-wedge.md) points at Dubai. If this channel produces demand, Portugal and Spain move ahead of `/moving-to-dubai` on [the landing-page backlog](../website/strategy.md). Let the channel decide the geography rather than defending the existing bet.

## What panel admission will require

A chartered firm putting a provider in front of a private client lends its regulated reputation. Expect real diligence on [insurance](../legal/liability.md), [terms](../legal/terms.md) and [compliance](../legal/compliance.md) — and "not yet" is a poor answer.

**That makes [the legal gate](../gameplan.md) a sales prerequisite, not only a delivery one.**

## Also worth noticing

**They may be customers as well as a channel.** Advisers specialising in UK departure are frequently internationally mobile themselves, with exactly the [qualifying profile](./launch-wedge.md).
