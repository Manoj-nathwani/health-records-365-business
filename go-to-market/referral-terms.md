# Referral terms and partner economics

## The fee is rarely why they refer

The intuitive model is that a big enough cut buys attention. It does not, and designing around the fee solves the wrong problem.

An adviser refers when three things hold, in this order:

1. **A client asked for something they could not provide.** No demand from their side of the desk means no referral, at any commission.
2. **They trust you not to embarrass them.** Their client relationship is their entire asset; introducing a third party risks it. This is the real gate.
3. **The economics are not insulting.**

Only the third is negotiable, and it is the least decisive. Some will decline a fee entirely and prefer to offer the service as unpaid added value — **that is a better outcome, not a failed negotiation**, because it means they see it as improving their own offer.

## The arithmetic, against the partner's own fee

Whether a fee registers depends entirely on what the partner charges. [Sojourner's Capital](./sojourners-capital.md) ran engagements at **£1,000 and £1,500** *(owner, stated 2026-08-20)*.

| Sale | 20% | 30% | Against a £1,000 engagement |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| £995 setup | £199 | £299 | **20–30% of what they earn from the client themselves** |
| £3,000 premium tier | £600 | £900 | More than half again, but out of proportion to the client's budget |

**At a firm this size the entry product's fee is material, not noise.** A few hundred pounds against a £1,000 engagement is real money, and it arrives for work they have already done.

The calculation inverts at Big-4-scale firms, where the same £199 is beneath notice. **Size the offer to the partner's own fee**, not to ours.

## Structures

| Structure | Good for | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed bounty per completed customer** | Simplest to explain and forecast; no revenue disclosure | Caps their upside, so it undersells the recurring product |
| **% of first-year revenue** | Standard, easily understood | One-off thinking for a business built on renewal |
| **% of all revenue while the customer stays** | Aligns them with retention, and is the most attractive offer available | Requires ongoing accounting and reporting to a third party |
| **Wholesale** — they buy at a discount and price it themselves | They keep the client relationship entirely; good where a firm wants to bundle | Loses direct contact with the customer, and with it the [retention loop](../commercial/retention-loop.md) |
| **Unpaid added value** | Costs nothing, and signals they believe in it | No financial hook if attention lapses |

**Offer ongoing revenue share for the first cohort of partners.** It is the most generous structure, it aligns them with the thing that actually matters, and generosity is cheap when the alternative is no distribution at all. The [membership model](../commercial/layers.md) makes it genuinely attractive: a share of a renewing membership is a recurring line for the partner, not a one-off bounty.

## What has to be settled

- **Whether regulated advisers can accept a fee at all.** CIOT and ICAEW members are subject to conduct rules on commissions and conflicts. Some categories of adviser will be unable to take one, which makes the unpaid-added-value route the default rather than the fallback. **Confirm this before proposing terms** — offering a fee somebody is not permitted to accept is a bad first impression.
- **Attribution.** How a customer is known to have come from a given partner, and what happens when they arrive months later having found you elsewhere.
- **Exclusivity.** Assume none, and do not offer it early.

## Why partners are not a substitute for selling

A profit share relocates the sales problem rather than removing it. **Signing a firm is B2B selling** — longer cycles, more stakeholders, diligence on [insurance](../legal/liability.md), [terms](../legal/terms.md) and [compliance](../legal/compliance.md), and a partner who agrees enthusiastically and then never mentions you. That last failure is the common one and it is silent: nothing is refused, nothing arrives.

And the offer being made is a percentage of an unproven service. **A share of nothing is nothing**, which any adviser works out immediately.

**Partners multiply proof. They do not create it** — see [the first customers](first-customers.md).

## Channel dependency

Distribution that is rented is not a moat. If every customer arrives through partners, they own the relationship and can renegotiate or stop. Keep at least one route that does not depend on anybody else, however small.
