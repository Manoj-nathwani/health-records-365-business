# Health Records 365 — Competitive Landscape & Strategic Implications

**Date:** 20 August 2026

## Executive summary

The competitive landscape is much broader than Chronicle Health.

There are already companies addressing different parts of the same underlying problem:

- provider-owned health records
- personal health record software
- AI over health records
- one-off medical-record reconstruction
- managed medical-record services
- patient advocacy and care coordination
- premium Medical Family Office services

This does **not** weaken the Health Records 365 thesis. In several areas, it strongly validates it.

The strongest strategic position is not another personal health record app, and not “Chronicle, but with AI and ongoing updates.”

It is:

> **Health Records 365 is a managed medical-history service. We obtain, organise and continuously maintain the medical history that no single doctor, hospital or country is responsible for.**

The most important competitive distinction is **responsibility**.

Healthcare providers manage their own records. Software products give customers tools to manage their own records. Health Records 365 should take responsibility for maintaining the customer's medical history across all of them.

---

## 1. Market structure

| Layer | Examples | Core customer job |
|---|---|---|
| Provider-owned records | NHS App, hospital portals, future NHS Single Patient Record | Show me what this healthcare system knows |
| DIY personal health records | Guava, KeepMD, RinVita, exora, Andaman7, PRIVAWELL, Across Medical | Give me software to organise my records |
| AI over health records | ChatGPT Health, Claude + HealthEx, Tempus olivia | Help me understand and question the records available to you |
| One-off reconstruction | Chronicle Health | Get my historical records and reconstruct them into something useful |
| Managed records | VITALL and similar services | Obtain and keep my records organised for me |
| Health advocacy / navigation | Solace and patient advocates | Help coordinate records, providers, follow-ups and care |
| Medical Family Office | SIP, Molliora, Delegata, Aperture and similar firms | Take responsibility for major parts of my healthcare life |
| **Health Records 365 opportunity** | **—** | **Take responsibility for continuity of my personal medical history** |

The opportunity is to occupy a clear **managed-history layer** between DIY software / one-off record reconstruction and expensive healthcare concierge services.

## 2. Chronicle Health

Chronicle Health remains the closest UK starting-point competitor.

Its service includes requesting full GP records, handling SAR administration and chasing, reconstructing a chronological health story, identifying unresolved or unclear history, preserving original sources and producing structured, searchable, AI-ready outputs.

Chronicle validates that:

- UK customers will pay hundreds of pounds for record reconstruction
- customers value having someone else deal with retrieval
- longitudinal reconstruction has value beyond document storage
- unresolved history and source provenance are useful outputs
- moving abroad is a legitimate acquisition trigger

Its standard service has been priced around **£399 one-off**.

Chronicle's current philosophy remains relatively file-based:

> retrieve → reconstruct → deliver → customer owns the files

However, Chronicle has also indicated potential hospital/specialist retrieval and discussion of ongoing or periodic updates.

### Strategic implication

Do **not** rely on:

> Chronicle is one-off and Health Records 365 is ongoing.

That is currently a difference, but not a defensible moat.

Likewise, do not position Health Records 365 as “Chronicle organises; we understand.” Chronicle already performs meaningful longitudinal reconstruction.

The stronger distinction is service philosophy and long-term responsibility.

---

## 3. RinVita and the international PHR category

RinVita is an important UK competitor because its positioning overlaps heavily with the international-health narrative.

Its proposition includes:

- portable medical history
- records from multiple countries
- family profiles
- timelines
- laboratory trends
- doctor-ready summaries
- multilingual support / translation
- record sharing
- expat and relocation use cases
- second opinions
- international and concierge healthcare

Pricing has been around **£39/month for an individual** and higher for family plans.

Other companies such as PRIVAWELL and Across Medical pursue similar ideas around portable records for internationally mobile individuals and families.

### What this invalidates as differentiation

The following are not unique:

- moving abroad
- medical passports
- translation
- one health record across countries
- family health profiles
- doctor-ready PDFs
- longitudinal laboratory views
- record sharing

These may still be valuable capabilities. They simply should not be treated as the moat.

### Strategic implication

> **Apps give you somewhere to manage your records. Health Records 365 manages them for you.**

---

## 4. VITALL — strongest validation of the managed-service model

VITALL is one of the most important comparables.

Its model combines:

- complete relevant medical-history acquisition
- Health Information Specialists
- provider-facing record requests
- ongoing record acquisition
- organisation and maintenance
- downloadable/shareable records

Its pricing has included approximately:

- **$79** for one record from one provider
- **$179** for history from one provider
- **$499** for a more complete relevant history
- **$49/month or $588/year** for ongoing membership

This is strikingly close to:

> **Build my record → Manage my record**

### What VITALL validates

- one-off reconstruction can cost hundreds
- ongoing medical-record management can be recurring
- roughly £30–£50/month is commercially credible
- human involvement is compatible with a software-enabled service

VITALL makes the fundamental business model **more credible**.

---

## 5. SIP Medical Family Office

SIP is one of the strongest validations of the broader international thesis.

It serves global citizens, expats, internationally mobile families and customers receiving care in several healthcare systems.

Its medical-record management includes:

- obtaining missing records
- collecting information from providers internationally
- organising records
- translation
- maintaining medical history
- physician review
- concise medical summaries
- supporting future medical care
- insurance / healthcare-navigation support

Published material has placed standalone medical-record management in the **thousands of euros per year**, with broader Medical Family Office offerings costing significantly more.

SIP also markets through **private client advisers**.

### What SIP validates

- internationally mobile affluent customers care about this problem
- people will pay significantly more than consumer-software prices
- adviser-led distribution is real
- translation and international-provider coordination have genuine demand

### Strategic implication

There may be a substantial **missing middle** between a £399 one-off reconstruction service and a £10,000–£50,000+ Medical Family Office relationship.

Health Records 365 may fit precisely into that gap.

## 6. Medical Family Offices

Several businesses show that wealthy customers already pay someone to take responsibility for health continuity.

Examples include:

- SIP
- Molliora
- Delegata
- Aperture
- My-Health UAE
- MARZ Medical
- PinnacleCare

These services can include medical-record management, specialist coordination, referrals, second opinions, international navigation, family health management, insurance support and health advocacy.

They are not direct competitors to a lower-priced managed-record service.

They validate a more fundamental behaviour:

> **wealthy people already pay another person or organisation to own healthcare continuity.**

Health Records 365 can take one valuable function from that world — the durable personal medical-history layer — and make it available at a much lower price.

| Basic DIY PHR | Free to ~$100/year |
| KeepMD-style product | ~$10/month |
| International PHR software | ~£39/month |
| Chronicle reconstruction | ~£399 one-off |
| VITALL history acquisition | ~$499 one-off |

| Premium managed medical records | Thousands/year |
| Medical Family Office | Many thousands to tens of thousands/year |

The bundles are not directly comparable, but the market structure suggests credible space between one-off reports and full concierge healthcare.

---

## 8. Pricing implications

The original hypothesis was roughly:

- £500–£1,000 initial setup
- £300–£600/year ongoing

The initial range looks credible.

The recurring service may be underpriced if it genuinely includes active retrieval, chasing missing records, reconciliation, QA, unresolved-item maintenance, incorporation of new healthcare and provider preparation.

Useful tests:

| Service | Price tests |
|---|---:|
| Initial record reconstruction | £795 / £995 / £1,495 |
| Annual managed history | £495 / £795 / £995 |
| High-touch / family / international | £1,500–£3,000+ |

These are tests, not final prices.

## 9. AI is rapidly commoditising product features

Guava, KeepMD, exora, Tempus olivia and others already provide combinations of:

- health-record aggregation
- timelines
- laboratory trends
- source-linked data
- family profiles
- sharing
- appointment preparation
- AI summaries
- conversational Q&A

ChatGPT Health and Claude + HealthEx are also moving directly into personal health records.

### Strategic implication

> **Ask your medical history**

is still an excellent experience, but it is not a moat.

The valuable question becomes:

> **Is the medical history being queried actually complete and trustworthy?**

---

## 10. AI makes the stewardship thesis stronger

An AI model can reason only over information available to it.

It cannot reason over:

- a hospital record never obtained
- pathology stuck in another system
- a Bangkok discharge note never supplied
- an MRI whose provider has been forgotten
- old private letters sitting in email
- failed investigations whose follow-up exists elsewhere

That moves the difficult problem upstream.

> **AI can reason over the records it has. Health Records 365 makes sure it has the right history.**

---

## 11. Open loops should become a process, not merely a feature

Identifying an unresolved item is useful, but competitors already do it.

The stronger opportunity is to maintain the **lifecycle** of unresolved history.

```text
Repeat test recommended
        ↓
No later result found
        ↓
OPEN ITEM CREATED
        ↓
Provider queried / customer informed
        ↓
New appointment happens
        ↓
Result supplied
        ↓
History updated
        ↓
ITEM CLOSED
```

The differentiation is not spotting an unresolved test once.

It is keeping track of unresolved medical-history threads until their documentary state changes.

---

## 12. Healthcare-system interoperability is improving

The NHS is developing the **Single Patient Record**.

Europe is progressing through the **European Health Data Space**.

There is also the **International Patient Summary (IPS)** standard.

Do not build the company on:

> healthcare systems will always be incapable of sharing records.

Instead:

> **No individual healthcare system owns someone's complete lifelong history across every provider, insurer and country.**

Improved interoperability should reduce collection costs. Every provider system becomes a source.

Health Records 365 remains the patient-centred continuity layer above them.

---

## 13. Portability should be intentional

Customers should eventually be able to use their Health Records 365 history with:

- ChatGPT
- Claude
- future healthcare AI products
- NHS systems
- European providers
- private specialists
- insurers
- international hospitals

> **The defensibility should come from the quality of stewardship, not captivity of the record.**

---

## 14. Privacy is a trust requirement, not the primary differentiator

Many competitors already emphasise customer ownership, downloads, local-first data, source retention and privacy.

> **Your records remain yours.**

is good messaging.

But customers pay for continuity, completeness, confidence, convenience and accountability.

---

## 15. Regulatory boundary

The initial product should stay firmly on the medical-history-management side:

- retrieving records
- factual organisation
- episode reconstruction
- source provenance
- documentary gaps
- record contradictions
- provider preparation
- unresolved-history status

It should avoid drifting casually into:

- diagnosis
- treatment selection
- individual medical recommendations
- deciding which tests a patient clinically needs

unless a properly designed clinician-reviewed and regulated layer is later developed.

Specialist UK privacy/regulatory advice should be obtained before handling customer records at production scale.

---

## 16. The UK-emigration wedge remains strong

Moving abroad remains a strong acquisition trigger, but not all emigrants have a valuable enough problem.

The stronger target is:

> **A financially comfortable British person with a meaningful existing medical history whose healthcare is about to cross a system boundary.**

The strongest combination is:

**trigger + history complexity + ability to pay**

Potential triggers include moving abroad, recently moving, seeing a new specialist, obtaining a second opinion, joining a new healthcare provider, starting private care, managing a parent's history, treatment overseas or returning to Britain after years abroad.

---

## 17. Dubai remains attractive

The UAE has a visible market for Medical Family Office and healthcare concierge services.

That signals that affluent Dubai-based customers already understand paying for healthcare coordination.

Health Records 365 can occupy a simpler layer:

> **We don't manage your doctors or practise medicine. We make sure your medical history is properly managed wherever those doctors happen to be.**

## 21. The core differentiation: accountability

The central difference is:

> **Here is software for managing your medical history.**

versus:

> **We manage your medical history.**

The second implies responsibility.

Someone is asking:

- Have all important sources been obtained?
- Did the hospital respond?
- Is the pathology report missing?
- Was the specialist appointment completed?
- Is this medication entry stale?
- Was the overseas discharge summary added?
- Does the new consultant need the whole archive or a focused pack?
- Has an open referral finally received an outcome?

That is the service.

## 23. Commercial architecture

The research increasingly suggests only three actual commercial products:

### 1. Build my medical history

Collect, retrieve, reconstruct and establish the baseline record.

### 2. Keep my medical history current

Ongoing retrieval, reconciliation, open-item maintenance and incorporation of new healthcare.

### 3. Prepare my medical history for care

Produce the relevant history for a new doctor, specialist, move abroad, second opinion, procedure or treatment abroad.

Everything else can primarily be an acquisition trigger or landing page.

## 24. Potential moat

| Potential advantage | Why it may compound |
|---|---|
| Accountable stewardship | Customer delegates the job rather than operating software |
| Active completeness | Know which sources exist and pursue missing material |
| Longitudinal reconciliation | Connect events across providers and years |
| Persistent unresolved-item lifecycle | Track open history until its status changes |
| Human QA | Preserve conflicts and uncertainty |
| Cross-system independence | NHS, private and overseas systems all become sources |
| Pre/post-care loop | Prepare before care and integrate outcomes afterwards |
| Record-retrieval expertise | Reusable operational IP |
| Distribution | Trusted advisers repeatedly introduce high-fit customers |
| Long customer relationship | A decade of maintained history becomes increasingly useful |
| Trust and reputation | Essential for sensitive personal information |

This is an:

**operations + relationship + trust + distribution moat**

Software makes it scalable. Software itself is not the moat.

---

## 25. Key commercial risk: human labour

The biggest unknown is:

> **How many human minutes are required to build and maintain an excellent medical history?**

The first paying customers should therefore double as an operational study.

Track separately:

- onboarding time
- record-request time
- chasing time
- document-processing time
- reconciliation time
- QA time
- customer-support time
- provider-pack preparation time
- recurring maintenance time

Only then automate the highest-cost repeatable workflows.

---

## 26. Negative validation: this is not for everyone

A healthy young person with little medical history may be perfectly served by a free PHR.

That supports a premium niche rather than a mass-market app.

Strongest early customer:

- meaningful medical history
- multiple providers
- transition underway
- values organisation
- willing to delegate
- able to pay

## 29. Recommended next validation phase

1. Deeply benchmark Chronicle, RinVita, VITALL and at least one Medical Family Office service.
2. Interview around ten real target customers with meaningful medical histories.
3. Interview around ten UK→UAE advisers across tax, relocation, wealth and insurance.
4. Manually deliver the full service to 5–10 paying customers.
5. Test initial setup prices around £795–£1,495.
6. Test annual management around £495–£995.
7. Record every human minute involved in delivery.
8. Observe which ongoing jobs customers actually ask for.
9. Obtain specialist UK data-protection and regulatory advice.
10. Build bespoke software only after costly and repeatable operational workflows are understood.

---

## 30. Final strategic conclusion

The competitive research makes the business **narrower and stronger**.

Health Records 365 should not attempt to become:

- another personal health record app
- another AI health assistant
- another medical passport
- a replacement for healthcare providers
- a full Medical Family Office

The strongest opportunity is:

> **a premium personal medical-history management service sitting between a one-off record pack and an expensive medical concierge.**

The company wins by taking responsibility for something no healthcare provider currently owns:

> **the continuity of the individual's medical history across providers, systems, years and countries.**

The customer is not paying for storage.

The customer is not paying for a chatbot.

The customer is paying for someone to make sure their medical history remains **complete, coherent, current and usable**.

That is the business Health Records 365 should build.
