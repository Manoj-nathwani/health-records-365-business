# Health Records 365 — Master Business Blueprint

**Domain:** health-records-365.com  
**Status:** Canonical business document  
**Working category:** Personal medical-history management across providers and countries  
**Core promise:** **Your medical history, managed wherever life takes you.**

---

## 1. Executive summary

Healthcare records are organised around healthcare providers, not around the person.

A GP holds one part of the story. Hospitals hold another. Private specialists, laboratories, screening providers, insurers and overseas providers may each hold separate pieces. Important context can also sit outside formal records: old letters, family history, vaccination records, genetic summaries, patient-reported history and documents the patient has kept themselves.

For someone with years or decades of healthcare behind them, there may never be one institution that holds the complete longitudinal story.

**Health Records 365 exists to become the continuity layer around the individual.**

We help customers:

1. **Collect** records from the places where care has happened.
2. **Preserve** the original evidence.
3. **Reconstruct** what happened over time.
4. **Connect** related events across providers and years.
5. **Reconcile** uncertainty, contradictions and changing clinical opinions.
6. **Surface** unresolved threads, missing outcomes and long-term patterns.
7. **Maintain** the history as new healthcare happens.
8. **Use** the history for future doctors, specialists, moves and treatment.

The business should feel like a **premium personal medical-history management service**, not a software subscription.

The customer should be able to say:

> **Health Records 365 manages my medical history.**

The initial commercial wedge is likely **British people leaving the UK**, particularly financially comfortable people with substantial medical histories who expect to use private or international healthcare. The long-term market is much broader: anyone whose healthcare is fragmented across providers, systems or countries.

The product is not fitness, wellness or biohacking. It is not primarily an NHS export service, document folder or AI chatbot. It is a service for preserving **continuity** when healthcare itself is fragmented.

## 2. The category we are building

The category to own is:

> **Personal medical-history management across providers and countries.**

A useful internal description is:

> **The personal health workspace for people whose healthcare spans countries and providers.**

That second phrase is useful for product thinking but should generally not lead customer-facing marketing.

The central idea is straightforward:

> **Healthcare is delivered encounter by encounter. Health Records 365 keeps track of the whole story.**

Healthcare providers will continue to change. Countries will change. Insurance will change. Hospitals will change. Technology will change.

**The patient's medical history should not start over each time.**

## 3. The problem

### Medical records are fragmented by design

A person's history may be spread across:

- current and previous GP practices
- NHS hospital trusts
- private hospitals
- consultants and specialists
- private laboratories
- imaging centres
- screening providers
- dental and optometry providers
- vaccination clinics
- insurers
- overseas hospitals and doctors
- genetic testing providers
- files already held by the patient
- information remembered by the patient but not formally recorded elsewhere

Even when every individual provider keeps good records, nobody necessarily owns the complete picture.

### A pile of documents is not a usable medical history

The hard part is not merely obtaining PDFs.

The hard part is understanding:

- what actually happened
- which events belong to the same health issue
- what was suspected versus confirmed
- whether a diagnosis was later questioned
- whether a referral actually led anywhere
- whether a recommended repeat test happened
- whether a failed or spoiled test was ever repeated
- how results changed over many years
- whether different laboratories were seeing the same pattern
- whether medication continued after its original rationale became unclear
- which historical information still matters
- what remains unresolved
- what a new doctor actually needs to know

### Moving country makes the problem obvious

Someone moving from the UK to Dubai, Spain, Portugal, Thailand or elsewhere cannot assume their NHS and private medical history will simply appear in the next healthcare system.

The practical result is often:

- a huge GP export
- folders of PDFs
- old consultant letters
- screenshots of results
- multiple patient portals
- partial hospital records
- new overseas records accumulating somewhere else
- important history remembered only by the patient

The patient becomes responsible for continuity without having the time, tools or expertise to manage it properly.

### The deeper failure is continuity, not filing

The motivating pattern behind this business is not simply “medical records are messy.”

It is that **no single person or organisation is continuously responsible for joining the story over time**.

Common failure modes include:

1. **Continuity failures** — follow-ups, referrals or planned tests disappear between encounters.
2. **Unresolved clinical history** — diagnoses are suspected, contradicted or left uncertain without the record clearly showing where things ended.
3. **Longitudinal blindness** — repeated findings across years, laboratories or providers are not viewed together.
4. **Patient-as-coordinator** — the patient has to remember what happened, chase records, explain contradictions and reconstruct their own history every time care changes.

This is the problem Health Records 365 is designed to own.

## 4. The proposition

### Your complete medical history, organised around you.

Health Records 365 brings together records from different providers and turns them into one coherent history that remains useful as doctors, hospitals and countries change.

### We collect it

We help bring together relevant information from NHS care, private healthcare, hospitals, laboratories, overseas providers and records the customer already holds.

### We make sense of it

We reconstruct the history across time rather than summarising documents one by one.

### We connect related care

Events recorded years apart or by different providers can be joined into the same health topic or episode where the evidence supports that connection.

### We preserve uncertainty

Suspected, historical, patient-reported and confirmed information are not silently flattened into one definitive list.

### We surface unfinished history

We identify follow-ups with no visible outcome, incomplete investigations, conflicting information and other open threads in the record.

### We keep it current

New consultations, tests and results become part of the same living history rather than another disconnected archive.

### We help customers use it

The customer can understand their past, prepare for appointments, move between providers and supply the right history when they receive care elsewhere.

## 6. The core operating loop

Internally, the service can be understood as:

**Collect → Preserve → Structure → Reconcile → Connect → Surface → Help use → Update**

### Collect
Bring together the raw record.

### Preserve
Keep original reports, letters, PDFs and source material.

### Structure
Organise information into meaningful health topics, episodes, results, medications, vaccinations and history.

### Reconcile
Identify conflicts, duplicate information, uncertain diagnoses and changing clinical opinions.

### Connect
Join information across providers and years when it relates to the same health thread.

### Surface
Make important unresolved or overlooked information visible.

### Help use
Prepare relevant information for appointments, specialists, second opinions, treatment or moves.

### Update
Incorporate the outcome back into the history so the record becomes more useful over time.

The recurring customer loop is even simpler:

> **Send us what happens next. We'll keep the history together.**

## 7. Open and unresolved health history

One of the highest-value concepts in the service is distinguishing between:

**something that was resolved**

and

**something that simply disappeared from the record.**

Examples include:

- a consultant recommended repeat testing but no later result is found
- a referral was made but no outcome appears
- a sample failed and there is no evidence it was repeated
- a diagnosis was entered but questioned in contemporaneous notes
- the same abnormal laboratory pattern appears across several years or providers
- a treatment continues while the original rationale becomes unclear
- a follow-up was expected but never recorded
- two providers hold contradictory information
- an investigation appears to have stopped midway

These should be treated as **open or unresolved record threads**, not automatically as clinical problems requiring intervention.

A core product idea is:

> **Health Records 365 notices when healthcare threads are opened but never clearly closed.**

The service's job is to make the state of the record clear.

## 8. Medical history contains uncertainty

Trust depends on not manufacturing certainty.

The record may contain:

### Clinically confirmed information
Diagnoses, procedures or findings clearly established in source records.

### Suspected or provisional diagnoses
Things clinicians considered but did not definitively confirm.

### Historical diagnoses of uncertain validity
Labels that remain in the record but were questioned, contradicted or never followed up.

### Patient-reported information
Symptoms, triggers, allergies or historical context supplied by the customer.

### Family history
Relevant information about relatives that may be scattered or incompletely documented.

### Genetic information
Supported genetic findings or consumer genetic summaries with clear provenance and limitations.

### Conflicting clinical opinions
Where clinicians disagreed, the disagreement should remain visible.

Health Records 365 should preserve these distinctions instead of silently “cleaning” the record into something more certain than the evidence supports.

## 11. The customer experience

The service should feel like having a trusted person or organisation maintain an important part of the customer's life.

### Step 1 — Give us what you have
The customer does not need to organise everything first.

### Step 2 — We help collect the rest
Where appropriate, Health Records 365 helps the customer obtain relevant records.

### Step 3 — We reconstruct the history
Information is connected across providers and time.

### Step 4 — We show uncertainty and gaps
Open threads, contradictions and missing outcomes become visible.

### Step 5 — The customer receives a usable history
Not merely an archive.

### Step 6 — We keep it current
New healthcare is incorporated as it happens.

### Step 7 — Use it when needed
For appointments, new specialists, moves, second opinions, overseas treatment and questions about the past.

The strongest emotional outcomes are **relief** and **control**.

Before:

> “I have years of records scattered everywhere and I don't really know what matters.”

After:

> “Someone has gone through it. It's organised. I understand what's outstanding. If a doctor needs something, I know it's there.”

And:

> “My healthcare may be spread across several organisations and countries, but my medical history isn't.”

Marketing should generally avoid fear. The service is about being organised, prepared and in control.

## 14. Core offerings

### 1. Organise My Medical History

**Who it is for:** anyone with years of fragmented NHS, private and personal medical information. Travel is not required.

**Problem:** “I have years of medical records but no clear idea what the complete story is.”

**Service:** bring everything together, reconstruct the history, preserve the originals and identify unresolved threads.

**Core proposition:**

> **Turn years of scattered medical records into one clear health history.**

### 2. Moving Abroad

**Who it is for:** British people preparing to leave the UK, initially potentially focused on destinations such as UAE/Dubai, Spain, Portugal, Thailand and Australia.

**Problem:** “My financial life is organised for the move, but what happens to decades of medical history?”

**Service:** collect and organise relevant UK history before departure, then continue incorporating new overseas care afterwards.

**Core proposition:**

> **Leaving the UK? Take your medical history with you.**

This is likely the strongest early acquisition wedge because the customer has a clear event and deadline.

### 3. Already Living Abroad

**Who it is for:** people who already left the UK and now have historic UK information plus new overseas healthcare.

**Problem:** “My old medical history is in Britain and my current healthcare is somewhere else.”

**Core proposition:**

> **Bring your old and new medical history back together.**

### 4. International Health Record Management

**Who it is for:** expatriates, nomads, international families, people splitting time between countries, people using treatment abroad, or people retaining UK specialists while living elsewhere.

**Problem:** “There may never be one health system responsible for my complete history.”

**Core proposition:**

> **One medical history across every provider and country.**

This is one of the strongest recurring-service propositions.

### 5. Ongoing Health Record Management

This is the recurring layer across the whole business.

Customers send new consultation letters, test results, reports, diagnoses, prescriptions and investigations. Health Records 365 incorporates them into the existing history.

**Core proposition:**

> **Send us what happens next. We'll keep the history together.**

### 6. Prepare for a New Doctor or Specialist

**Trigger:** joining a new GP, seeing a specialist, obtaining a second opinion, undergoing a procedure or attending a private health assessment.

Health Records 365 prepares the relevant subset of the customer's history rather than handing every provider the entire archive.

**Core proposition:**

> **Give your next doctor the history that actually matters.**

### 7. Treatment or Healthcare Abroad

**Trigger:** the customer plans to receive healthcare in another country.

The service helps clarify what has already happened, what remains unresolved, which records are relevant to the planned care and what outcomes need to be incorporated afterwards.

Healthcare-provider research and navigation may sit around this service where appropriate.

**Core proposition:**

> **Arrive with your history ready. Bring the outcome back with you.**

### 8. Family Medical Record Management

A likely later expansion for people helping manage the history of a spouse, elderly parent or other family member, with appropriate permissions.

**Core proposition:**

> **Help your family keep the full medical picture together.**

## 18. Why customers pay

Customers pay for a combination of:

### Time
They do not want to manually read and organise decades of records.

### Expertise
Most people do not understand the structure of medical records, coding, episodes of care or how to reconcile contradictory information.

### Continuity
They want their history to survive changes in doctor, provider and country.

### Confidence
They want to know that important information has not quietly disappeared between organisations.

### Convenience
When a doctor asks for something, they can find or prepare it quickly.

### Preparation
The right information can be assembled before a specialist, second opinion or overseas treatment.

### Ongoing maintenance
They do not want today's clean archive to become tomorrow's mess.

The customer is not paying to store PDFs. They are paying for someone to keep the story coherent.

## 22. Early validation plan

Do not spend months building a large product before proving willingness to pay and partner demand.

A sensible first validation target is:

- speak to enough UK-emigration advisers to find roughly **five** who are genuinely willing to refer customers
- manually deliver the service for roughly **ten** early customers
- test a setup price in the region of **£500–£1,000**
- observe how much work record retrieval and reconstruction actually requires
- measure which outputs customers value most
- test whether they naturally send new records afterwards
- test willingness to pay for ongoing management

The first customers can be delivered with a high-touch concierge workflow. Automation should be added where real service delivery creates repetitive work, rather than designing the entire system in advance.

The key validation is not “do people like the idea?”

It is:

> **Will people pay us to take responsibility for keeping their medical history coherent over time?**

## 27. Data sovereignty, privacy and trust

Health information is among the most sensitive information customers possess. Data control should be a major trust advantage.

### Core principle

> **Your medical history should not be locked to Health Records 365, our software or a foreign jurisdiction.**

### Default hosting

A sensible default is UK/London-hosted infrastructure for customer records, subject to the final technical and compliance design.

### Portability

Customers should be able to export their complete record.

### Flexible hosting

Where commercially and operationally appropriate, other data-location arrangements or customer-controlled infrastructure can be supported.

### Preserve source evidence

Original documents remain available rather than being replaced by generated summaries.

### Traceability

Important derived information should be traceable back to its source.

### Preserve uncertainty

Suspected diagnoses must not become confirmed diagnoses simply because a system summarised them.

### Keep conflicts visible

When the historical record contains disagreement, the service should show it rather than manufacturing a clean answer.

### Customer control

> **Your records remain yours.**

The business should be defensible because of trust, relationship and maintained continuity — not because customers are trapped.

## 28. AI positioning

Modern AI can be heavily used underneath the service without becoming the product story.

Customer-facing language should generally be:

> **Modern technology helps us organise and understand complex medical histories while preserving the original records and their context.**

The customer does not need to know which language model is used.

Specific vendors, model routing, embeddings and inference architecture are implementation details unless they materially affect privacy, jurisdiction or contractual terms.

Human oversight should be retained where it materially improves accuracy, trust or safety.

The strongest visible benefit of AI is not “AI-powered healthcare.”

It is the ability to naturally ask useful questions of a complete, carefully maintained history.

## 29. Regulatory and clinical boundary

Health Records 365 should initially stay on the conservative side of the line between **record management** and **clinical decision-making**.

Appropriate initial functions include:

- retrieving information from the customer's own record
- summarising historical events
- showing what a clinician previously recommended
- identifying that no later outcome is visible in the available record
- comparing records over time
- surfacing contradictions
- preparing information for appointments
- preparing provider packs
- explaining what the historical record says
- supporting general provider research or navigation
- helping a customer formulate questions for their clinician

Example of safer wording:

> **“Your consultant recommended a repeat test and no later result is visible in the records we have.”**

Example of a materially different claim:

> **“You should get a CT scan.”**

The second is a personalised clinical recommendation and should not be casually folded into the record-management product.

If the business later wants to provide personalised clinical recommendations, this should be designed deliberately, potentially with clinician review and a proper assessment of UK medical-device and professional-regulatory obligations based on intended purpose.

The service should not position itself as a replacement for doctors.

## 30. Competitive landscape

Health Records 365 should assume that personal health records, record reconstruction and AI-assisted medical-history tools are existing categories.

The opportunity is not based on claiming nobody else has tried to organise medical records.

### Chronicle Health — closest current UK benchmark

**Research snapshot: 20 August 2026. Pricing and features may change.**

Chronicle Health is the closest current UK competitor and is substantially more capable than a simple NHS export service.

Its service includes longitudinal reconstruction, source citations, open items, contradictions and gaps, private laboratory information, genetics summaries, moving-abroad positioning and treatment-abroad use cases.

Its delivered **Chronicle Pack** includes components such as:

- Health Story
- Current Care
- Record Summary
- Clinical Timeline
- Topic References
- Bigger Picture
- About This Pack
- original source records
- `ai_context.md`

The `ai_context.md` output is intended to provide portable context that can be used with external systems such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

Chronicle accepts inputs including NHS/private GP records, private consultations, hospital documents, diagnostics, executive health assessments, laboratory results, dental/optometry information, family history and genetics summaries.

At the time of research, Chronicle's commercial model was primarily a **one-off reconstruction and delivery service** rather than an ongoing hosted account or managed living record. It delivered an encrypted archive and stated that its retained processing copy would be deleted within a limited period. Future updates were a separate engagement or could be managed by the customer themselves.

At the time of research, pricing observed included approximately:

- standard service: **£399**
- promotional price: **£199** through 31 August 2026
- earlier indexed promotion: **£299**
- existing-record processing: **£99**
- gift product: **£399**

This pricing appears to be evolving and should be treated as a dated market snapshot rather than a permanent benchmark.

Chronicle also had a dedicated **Moving Abroad** page, meaning the UK-emigration use case is validated but not unique.

Its hospital/specialist retrieval capability was not yet fully launched at the time of research, and it could organise overseas records the customer already possessed but did not position itself as retrieving records directly from overseas providers.

Chronicle also maintains a conservative regulatory boundary: it does not present itself as providing diagnosis, prognosis, treatment recommendations or medical advice.

### The meaningful distinction

Do **not** position the market as:

> “Chronicle organises records; Health Records 365 understands them.”

That would be inaccurate. Chronicle already performs substantial interpretation, reconciliation and open-item analysis.

The intended strategic distinction is closer to:

**Chronicle:**

> **Reconstruct and deliver the medical record.**

**Health Records 365:**

> **Reconstruct it, then continue managing it with you as healthcare changes.**

The long-term loop is:

**new provider → new records → updated history → next appointment → next country → next provider**

repeated over years.

### Other relevant competitors / adjacent products

- Patients Know Best
- Guava
- Andaman7
- KeepMD
- WellArrive
- RinVita
- MediPass
- OwnChart

Some of these are portals, personal health records, portable medical records or data platforms rather than direct service competitors. OwnChart is particularly relevant conceptually because of provenance/source/fact/event-style architecture.

The strategic conclusion is important:

> **“Upload records + chat” is not a premium moat.**

The moat must come from long-term managed continuity, trust, maintained history, customer service and the operating workflow around real healthcare events.

## 35. Current technical/product decisions

Technology is deliberately **not the current strategic focus**. The first goal is to prove the service and workflow.

One important decision has already been made: **do not base the product on OpenKnowledge/Inkeep's OpenKnowledge interface.**

OpenKnowledge was useful for validating the interaction model — files/folders, readable content and chat — but the interface is not sufficiently clean or controllable for the intended premium customer experience.

Building the customer surface directly gives:

- complete control over UX
- no dependence on a GPL codebase for the product UI
- a cleaner service-first experience
- a simpler privacy and data-location story
- freedom to design health-native views rather than developer-oriented knowledge tooling

The useful paradigm should be retained:

> **durable, understandable source material + structured history + conversational access**

without making OpenKnowledge part of the product or business plan.

A conceptual portable record may eventually contain things equivalent to:

- knowledge / narrative history
- structured data
- source documents
- metadata / provenance

But those are implementation details, not customer messaging.
