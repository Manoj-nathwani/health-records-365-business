# Prepare my history for care

**Give your next doctor the history that actually matters.**

---

## The problem with handing over everything

A new consultant has twenty minutes and you have thirty years of records. Give them all of it and they will read none of it. Give them nothing and they start from your memory, which is where the errors come from.

What is useful is the part that concerns them — assembled, in order, with the open questions in their area stated plainly.

---

## When people use this

- **Registering with a new doctor**, in a new country or at home
- **Seeing a specialist** about something with a long history behind it
- **Getting a second opinion**, where what the first opinion rested on matters
- **Having a procedure**, where previous investigations, allergies and medications need to be in front of the team
- **Treatment abroad**, and bringing the outcome back into your record afterwards

---

## What you get

A prepared extract for that appointment:

- The relevant history, in order, with the original documents attached
- Results that bear on this specialty, over time rather than as single readings
- Current medications, allergies and anything that changes what is safe to prescribe
- The open questions in that area — what has been asked and not answered

Written to be read by a clinician who has never met you and cannot look anything up.

---

## And afterwards

What comes out of the appointment goes back into your history — the letter, the results, the plan. That is the half people forget, and it is the reason a record slowly stops matching reality.
