## What actually happens to your records when you leave

Nothing. That is the problem.

Your GP record stays with a practice you are about to leave. Hospital records stay with the trusts that made them. Private consultations sit with individual clinics who have no obligation to keep in touch. Nothing is forwarded, nothing is consolidated, and no system tells your new doctor abroad that any of it exists.

You can download a summary from the NHS App. It is worth having and it is not your history — it holds what one system knows, in coded form, without the hospital letters, the private tests, or anything from before your practice went digital.

---

## Why it gets worse after you go

Two things happen at once.

**The old history gets harder to reach.** Requesting records from a UK provider while living in Bali is not impossible, but it involves post, identity checks and follow-up calls in a timezone eight hours away. Most people put it off, and putting it off is how a five-year gap starts.

**The new history starts accumulating somewhere else.** A clinic in Dubai, a hospital in Bangkok, a laboratory in Lisbon. Each holds its own piece and none of them talks to the others or to anything in Britain.

Within a few years you have two disconnected halves and no single doctor who has seen both.

---

## The thing people find, when they look

Almost everyone who has their history reconstructed finds something unfinished in it.

A test that was recommended and never booked. A referral with no recorded outcome. A result that came back abnormal and was followed up by a letter to an old address.

Those things do not resolve themselves, and leaving the country makes them permanent. **This is generally the last convenient moment to find them.**

---

## What we do

**Before you go**, we request your records from every UK provider who holds them — GP practices past and present, hospital trusts, private consultants, laboratories — and reconstruct them into a history a doctor in any country can read.

**After you go**, we keep handling it. Your new providers become sources like any other, your history stays one thing instead of two — and when you need to be seen, we research the facilities where you have landed, book the appointment, and prepare your history so the new doctor takes it seriously from the first visit.

## Timing

**Start two to three months before you fly**, if you can. Records take that long to arrive and chase, and being in the country makes identity checks simpler.

If you are leaving sooner than that, it is still worth starting. **And if you have already gone, this is still very much possible** — it is just slower.

## Is this worth it for you?

**Probably, if** you have a real amount of history behind you — a condition being monitored, past investigations, medication, several providers over the years — and you are going somewhere you will be paying for your own care.

**Probably not, if** you are in your twenties, have seen one GP, and have never been referred anywhere. Take the NHS App summary and save your money.
