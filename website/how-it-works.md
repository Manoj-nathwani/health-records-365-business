# How we build a medical history

Most of what people call a medical record is a folder of documents. A history is different: it says what happened, in what order, which events belong together, and what was never finished.

Getting from one to the other is the work.

---

## 1. We map what should exist

Before requesting anything, we work out what there is to request.

That is a conversation, and it takes longer than people expect. Most of us cannot list our own care: the practice you were with at university, the walk-in clinic abroad, the private consultant you saw once in 2014. We work backwards from the records we can get to find the ones you have forgotten — a referral letter names a hospital, a discharge summary names a consultant, a result names a laboratory.

The output is a list of every source that should hold something about you, and its status.

**Why it matters:** a history is only as complete as the list it was built from. This is the step that decides whether anything is missing, and it is the one nobody does for themselves.

---

## 2. We request all of it

Formal requests go to every provider on that list — NHS practices and trusts, private hospitals, consultants, laboratories, imaging centres, clinics abroad.

Some respond in weeks. Some respond with the wrong thing. Some do not respond at all, and those are the ones that matter, because they are also the ones you would have given up on.

**We chase them.** That is most of the work, and it is the part that makes this a service rather than a piece of software.

---

## 3. We reconstruct what happened

Documents arrive out of order, in different formats, using different words for the same thing. Turning them into a history means deciding what belongs with what.

- **Events get connected.** A symptom in 2019, a referral in 2020 and a procedure in 2021 may be one story told across three providers who never spoke to each other.
- **Contradictions stay visible.** Where two clinicians disagreed, we show the disagreement. We do not pick the more recent one and quietly drop the other.
- **Uncertainty stays uncertain.** *Considered* does not become *diagnosed*. A suspected condition that was never confirmed is recorded as exactly that, however tempting it is to tidy it into something definite.
- **Every claim keeps its source.** Anything we say about your history links to the document it came from, so you or your doctor can check it.

---

## 4. We surface what was never finished

This is the part people find most useful, and it is the reason a history is worth more than an archive.

As the record comes together, certain things become visible that no single provider could see:

- A test recommended, with no later result anywhere
- A referral made, with no outcome recorded
- A sample that failed, and no evidence it was repeated
- The same abnormal reading appearing across years and different laboratories
- Medication continuing long after the reason for starting it became unclear

**We do not tell you what these mean.** We tell you the record contains them, and that nothing in it shows how they ended. What to do is a conversation with a doctor — but you cannot have that conversation about something you do not know is there.

---

## 5. We hand it over

You get the history, the original documents, and the ability to give any part of it to any doctor, in any country.

It is yours. Export it whenever you like, in a form that does not require us to be involved.

---

## 6. And then we handle what comes next

A history is accurate on the day it is finished and starts decaying immediately — and most histories contain things that still need doing.

Membership is both halves of that. You send us what happens and we keep the record true. And when something needs doing — the test that was never booked, the specialist you have been meaning to see, a check-up in a country you moved to last year — we research where, book it, prepare your history for the appointment, and bring the results home. The unfinished items stay tracked until something actually closes them.

## How long this takes

**Weeks to begin, months to finish.** We start within days of the first conversation, but providers respond on their own timescales and some need chasing more than once.

We would rather tell you this now than apologise for it later. Anyone promising a complete medical history in a fortnight is either not requesting much or not chasing anything.
