# About Health Records 365

---

## Why this exists

It started with trying to answer a simple question about my own health and finding that nobody could.

My records were spread across the NHS App, a hospital portal, a patient-records platform, private clinics in three countries and my own email. No GP had a continuous picture. When I finally put it together, I found a colonoscopy I had been waiting five years for had been quietly cancelled — the letter went to an address I had left eighteen months earlier, and being removed from a waiting list generates no phone call.

I also found a recheck that had been agreed and never happened, a test from 2019 that was never processed, and a diagnosis in my record that the diagnosing consultant had written was probably wrong.

None of that was anybody's fault. It is what happens when care is delivered one appointment at a time and nobody is responsible for the whole.

---

## Who I am

**Manoj Nathwani.** I am a British software engineer and I have spent most of my career building health systems — including a national telemedicine service in Rwanda, and work with NHS England, the World Health Organization, UNAIDS, the Gates Foundation and several ministries of health.

I mention that for one reason. **If somebody who builds health infrastructure for a living cannot keep their own medical history straight, the problem is not that patients are disorganised.**

---

## What we are trying to be

Not a health app. Not an AI assistant. Not a folder of PDFs with a search box.

The thing we are trying to be is simpler and harder: **somebody whose job it is to keep your medical history complete, coherent and current** — across every doctor you see, every country you live in, and every year that passes.

Your providers manage their records. We manage your history.

---

## How we work

By hand, mostly, and deliberately.

Every record is built by a person who reads the documents, works out what connects to what, and chases what has not arrived. We use good tools, but the judgement is human, and somebody is accountable for whether your record is right.

That is not a scalable-sounding thing to say. It is the truthful one.
