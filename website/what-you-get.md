# What you actually receive

A medical history you can read, and that a doctor can use.

Below is a real one, with the identifying details changed. It is the clearest way to explain this, so we would rather show it than describe it.

**[Open the example →]**

---

## The current summary

One page. What is going on with your health now, what is being monitored, what a doctor seeing you today would need to know first.

This is the page you send ahead of an appointment.

---

## The history

What has happened to you, in order, written as something readable rather than a table of codes.

Events that belong together are grouped together, even when they happened years apart at different hospitals. Where a story ended, it says so. Where it did not, it says that too.

---

## Unresolved items

Everything the record opens and never closes:

- Tests recommended but not recorded as done
- Referrals with no outcome
- Investigations that appear to stop midway
- Repeat findings nobody has looked at together
- Contradictions between providers

Each one says what the record shows and what it does not show. None of them tells you what to do about it.

---

## Results over time

The same measurement across years and different laboratories, in one place, with each result kept against the reference range printed on its own report.

Single results are often unremarkable. The pattern across a decade is where the information is.

---

## Medications, vaccinations, allergies

Current and historical, with the context of why each was started where the record shows it.

Vaccination history matters more than people expect once they are living abroad, and it is one of the things most commonly lost in a move.

---

## Family and genetic background

Where it is relevant and you have it — what runs in your family, and anything from genetic testing, recorded with its limitations rather than overstated.

---

## Every original document

Every letter, report and result we obtained, kept exactly as it arrived.

Nothing in your history is a summary you have to take on trust. Each statement links to the document underneath it, so you or your doctor can check any of it in a couple of clicks.

---

## Prepared extracts, when you need them

A new specialist does not want three decades of records. They want the part that concerns them.

When you are seeing someone new, we prepare that — the relevant history, the relevant results, and the open questions in their area.

**[Preparing for care →]**

## In a format that outlives us

Plain, readable files. Not a login, not an app, not a database only we can open.

You can hand the whole thing to a doctor on a memory stick, email one section, or keep a copy somewhere we have no access to. If Health Records 365 disappeared tomorrow, your history would still open and still make sense.
