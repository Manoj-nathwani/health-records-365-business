# The problem

Source: [Master Business Blueprint](../Inbox/health-records-365-business-blueprint.md) §3.

## Medical records are fragmented by design

A person's history may be spread across:

- current and previous GP practices
- NHS hospital trusts
- private hospitals
- consultants and specialists
- private laboratories
- imaging centres
- screening providers
- dental and optometry providers
- vaccination clinics
- insurers
- overseas hospitals and doctors
- genetic testing providers
- files already held by the patient
- information remembered by the patient but not formally recorded elsewhere

Even when every individual provider keeps good records, nobody necessarily owns the complete picture.

## A pile of documents is not a usable medical history

The hard part is not merely obtaining PDFs. The hard part is understanding:

- what actually happened
- which events belong to the same health issue
- what was suspected versus confirmed
- whether a diagnosis was later questioned
- whether a referral actually led anywhere
- whether a recommended repeat test happened
- whether a failed or spoiled test was ever repeated
- how results changed over many years
- whether different laboratories were seeing the same pattern
- whether medication continued after its original rationale became unclear
- which historical information still matters
- what remains unresolved
- what a new doctor actually needs to know

## Moving country makes the problem obvious

Someone moving from the UK to Dubai, Spain, Portugal, Thailand or elsewhere cannot assume their NHS and private medical history will simply appear in the next healthcare system.

The practical result is often a huge GP export, folders of PDFs, old consultant letters, screenshots of results, multiple patient portals, partial hospital records, new overseas records accumulating somewhere else, and important history remembered only by the patient.

The patient becomes responsible for continuity without having the time, tools or expertise to manage it properly.

## The deeper failure is continuity, not filing

The motivating pattern is not simply "medical records are messy." It is that **no single person or organisation is continuously responsible for joining the story over time**.

Four failure modes:

1. **Continuity failures** — follow-ups, referrals or planned tests disappear between encounters.
2. **Unresolved clinical history** — diagnoses are suspected, contradicted or left uncertain without the record clearly showing where things ended.
3. **Longitudinal blindness** — repeated findings across years, laboratories or providers are not viewed together.
4. **Patient-as-coordinator** — the patient has to remember what happened, chase records, explain contradictions, reconstruct their own history and organise their own access every time care changes.

This is the problem Health Records 365 is designed to own — modes 1 and 2 through [the open-loop lifecycle](../product/open-loops.md), mode 3 through the reconstruction itself, and mode 4 through [navigation](../product/navigation.md).
