# Record retrieval playbook

**Status: not yet written.** This is the core operational IP of the business and the largest single gap in the knowledge base.

## What this doc must answer

**Per source type** — GP practice, NHS hospital trust, private hospital, private consultant, private laboratory, imaging centre, overseas provider:

- What is the legal mechanism for requesting on a customer's behalf, and what authority does it require? See [customer mandate](../legal/customer-mandate.md).
- What exactly do we send — form, letter, portal, email?
- What is the statutory or practical response window?
- What arrives, and in what format?
- What does it cost, if anything?
- What do we do when nothing comes back? Escalation path and stopping rule.

**Cross-cutting:**

- How overseas retrieval differs, and where it is not practical at all
- Which sources are cheap and which are unbounded work — this drives [the cost model](./cost-model.md)
- Where [improving interoperability](../strategy/interoperability.md) will reduce this work

## Why it blocks

Everything. No retrieval procedure means no [SOP](./delivery-sop.md), no cost estimate, and no service.

> The stopping rule matters as much as the procedure. Chasing an unresponsive trust is unbounded work, and [the moat and the cost driver are the same activity](../go-to-market/labour-economics.md).

## How to write it

Run it on the founder's own history first and document what actually happened, rather than researching it in the abstract. Verify the legal mechanics with the specialist advice in step 9 of the [validation plan](../go-to-market/validation-plan.md) before relying on them.
