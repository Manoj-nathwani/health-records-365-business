# The long-term retention loop

Sources: [Master Business Blueprint](../Inbox/health-records-365-business-blueprint.md) §37; [Competitive Research](../Inbox/health-records-365-competitive-research.md) §20.

Health Records 365 becomes more valuable the longer it maintains a customer's history.

A worked example:

1. A customer joins while leaving the UK.
2. Health Records 365 reconstructs decades of UK healthcare.
3. Six months later, a Dubai consultation is added.
4. Then private blood tests.
5. Then a procedure in Bangkok.
6. Then a specialist in London.
7. Then a move to Spain.

**Health Records 365 remains the constant.**

The relationship can last for years or decades.

## Recurrence is not the differentiator

"Ongoing updates by themselves" is [table stakes](../market/table-stakes.md) — [VITALL](../market/competitors/vitall.md) sells a ~$49/month managed membership and Chronicle has signalled periodic updates.

The compounding effect above is a *consequence* of good stewardship, not a substitute for it. What makes a long relationship valuable is that someone is [accountable for completeness](../market/differentiation.md) throughout it. A decade of passively accumulated documents compounds nothing.

## What the loop depends on

Customers must actually forward new records. Whether that habit forms is one of the sharpest [open questions](../go-to-market/open-questions.md), and should be measured directly during [validation](../go-to-market/validation-plan.md).
