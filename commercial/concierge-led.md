# Concierge-led selling

> **Hypothesis: [navigation](../product/navigation.md) is the biggest sell, and the record is the engine underneath it — not the other way round.**

Not yet a decision. This doc holds the argument, the risks, and the cheap test.

## Why the concierge probably leads

**People buy relief, not homework.** "We organise your records" asks the customer to care about filing. "We found the right gastroenterologist in Bangkok, booked it, prepared your history, and the results are already home" is a story about their life getting easier. The [emotional outcome](../product/customer-experience.md) was always relief and control — the concierge *is* that outcome; the record is how it is produced.

**It changes who we are compared with.** Lead with the record and the anchor is [Chronicle at £399](../market/competitors/chronicle-health.md) — we look expensive. Lead with the concierge and the anchor is [SIP and the medical family offices](../market/competitors/sip-medical-family-office.md) at thousands a year — we look like remarkable value. **Same service, opposite anchoring.** This is [the missing middle](../market/missing-middle.md) entered from above instead of below.

**The record is invisible until it fails; navigation is felt every time.** A perfectly maintained history produces no experience. A booked appointment, a script for the consultation, results chased and filed — each is a visible act of service, and visible acts are what renewals and referrals are made of.

**The adviser sentence improves.** *"We handle the financial side of your move; they handle the healthcare side"* is stronger than *"they organise medical records"* — it makes us the [Sojourner's](../go-to-market/sojourners-capital.md) of health, a category the adviser already understands because they are in it.

**The strongest trigger is a navigation trigger.** [The customer mid-crisis abroad](../go-to-market/first-customers.md) — a scare, a foreign system, no idea where to go — is buying navigation *first* and discovering they need the record to make it work.

**Founder-market fit is embarrassing in its directness.** The [reference implementation's](../operations/reference-implementation.md) active threads are literally a multi-city care itinerary with bookings, scripts, fallback providers and dependency ordering. The founder has already delivered this product once, to himself, unpaid.

## What stays true regardless

**The record remains the moat.** A concierge without the reconciled history is a booking agency — a commodity that competes with hotel-desk medicine and expat Facebook groups. Every distinctive act of navigation — *sequence the draw before stopping the supplement, insist on keeping the paraffin blocks* — comes out of the record. **Sell the concierge; the record is step one of delivering it.**

So the build order does not change. The *pitch* changes.

## What it would restructure

**Pricing architecture tilts from products to membership.** The natural concierge model is a family-office-lite:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Onboarding** | Build the record — framed as *"we cannot plan your care without knowing your history"* | ~£995, as now |
| **Membership** | Record kept current **+ navigation on demand** | The [£795–£995+ annual](./pricing.md), with a stronger case for the upper end |
| **Premium** | Family, multi-country, high-touch | £1,500–3,000+, and now with obvious content |

The record build stops being *the product* and becomes *the initiation fee* — which also defuses the Chronicle comparison entirely, because nobody compares an initiation fee to a rival's whole product.

**Positioning stretches without breaking.** [One job](../strategy/principles.md) was "manage the person's medical history." The concierge framing widens it to **"someone is handling my healthcare"** — which is the same [accountability](../market/differentiation.md) promise with a bigger surface. *There is finally someone whose job it is* survives intact.

## The risks, honestly

**Operational shape changes.** Records work batches; concierge work is reactive, real-time and timezone-scattered. A solo founder can batch. Whether he can also be responsive across Bali–London–Bangkok simultaneously is a real question — and the reason to bound the offer:

> ⚠️ **Planned and semi-planned care only. This is not an emergency assistance service.** No 24/7 line, no medical emergencies, no evacuation — that is International SOS and the insurers' assistance arms, a different industry with different economics. Say so on the site, explicitly, before the first customer assumes otherwise.

**Crisis customers convert fastest and are hardest to serve well.** The mid-crisis trigger sells the concierge, but a customer in crisis needs speed a one-person operation may not have. First cohort: lead with *planned* navigation — tests, check-ups, specialists, elective procedures, the moving-abroad sequence.

**Scope creep is the failure mode.** "Can you also talk to my insurer / translate this / come with me" — each yes is billable-feeling and unbounded. The [later-capabilities](../product/later-capabilities.md) discipline applies: demand from real customers sets what gets added.

**Independence has to survive the pitch.** Concierge industries run on commissions; ours [structurally cannot](../product/navigation.md). That is a selling point — *"we take nothing from any provider we send you to"* — but it also means the margin must live entirely in the fee, which is another argument for the membership tilt.

## The cheap test

The first three to five customers are the experiment — **pitch both framings and watch which one closes:**

1. *"We build and manage your complete medical history"* — record-led
2. *"We handle your healthcare wherever you are — research, bookings, preparation — built on a complete record of your history"* — concierge-led

Also decisive, and free: [the Sojourner's question](../go-to-market/sojourners-capital.md) — *"what do clients ask you about that you have nowhere to send them?"* If the answer sounds like "finding doctors abroad" rather than "getting records out", the hypothesis is confirmed from the demand side before we sell anything.

## What confirms, what refutes

| Signal | Reads as |
| --- | --- |
| Prospects light up at bookings/itineraries, glaze at records | Confirmed — reposition the site's hero accordingly |
| Prospects want the pack, decline ongoing navigation | Refuted — record-led stands, navigation stays premium-tier content |
| Advisers describe unmet demand in navigation terms | Confirmed for the [partner channel](../go-to-market/partners-and-distribution.md) at least |
| First customers use navigation once, then only the record | Membership pricing wrong — unbundle |
