# Initial target customer

Source: [Master Business Blueprint](../Inbox/health-records-365-business-blueprint.md) §16.

## The strongest early customer

- British
- financially comfortable
- substantial medical history
- leaving or recently left the UK
- likely to use private healthcare
- likely to interact with several providers
- someone who values having important personal affairs properly organised
- willing to pay for trusted service rather than administer everything personally

People in their 50s, 60s and beyond are commercially attractive: more likely to have decades of history, multiple ongoing health threads, more healthcare usage and greater perceived downside if information is missing.

**But Health Records 365 should not brand itself as an over-50s service.** Age is a targeting variable, not the identity of the business.

## Negative validation — this is not for everyone

**From the [competitive research](../Inbox/health-records-365-competitive-research.md) §26.** A healthy young person with little medical history may be perfectly served by a free personal health record. That is fine: it supports a **premium niche** rather than a mass-market app.

The qualifying combination is **trigger + history complexity + ability to pay** — all three. See [the launch wedge](../go-to-market/launch-wedge.md).

Strongest early customer:

- meaningful medical history
- multiple providers
- transition underway
- values organisation
- willing to delegate
- able to pay

"Willing to delegate" is the one most easily missed. Someone who enjoys managing their own records is a poor fit for a service sold on [taking responsibility](./differentiation.md).

## The broader UK market

A customer does not need to emigrate for fragmentation to exist.

> **Your healthcare may already be spread across providers even if you've never left Britain.**

This keeps the long-term market broad while allowing "moving abroad" to remain a focused acquisition wedge. The wedge is set out in the [launch hypothesis](../go-to-market/launch-wedge.md).
