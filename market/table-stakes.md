# Table stakes

Source: [Competitive Research](../Inbox/health-records-365-competitive-research.md) §§3, 9, 20.

These capabilities are useful and mostly worth building. **None of them is defensibility.** Competitors already ship them, and AI is commoditising the rest rapidly.

- AI chat
- longitudinal timeline
- lab trends
- source citations
- provenance
- original-document storage
- medical passport
- translation
- family profiles
- emergency summary
- provider packs
- exportability
- data ownership
- cross-border portability
- moving-abroad use case
- AI summaries
- DICOM storage
- detecting a single open loop
- **ongoing updates by themselves**

## Why this list matters

These are the capabilities most easily mistaken for a position. Two in particular:

- **Moving abroad.** [Chronicle](./competitors/chronicle-health.md) has a Moving Abroad page; [RinVita](./competitors/rinvita.md) is built on expat and relocation use cases. It remains an excellent **acquisition trigger** — just not a position.
- **Ongoing record management.** [VITALL](./competitors/vitall.md) already sells a $49/month managed membership. "We keep it current" is a difference from Chronicle, not a moat.

## The rule

Build table stakes because customers expect them. Sell on [accountability](./differentiation.md).

> **Ask your medical history** is still an excellent experience, but it is not a moat. The valuable question becomes: **is the medical history being queried actually complete and trustworthy?**
