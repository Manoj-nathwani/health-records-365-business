# Regulatory and clinical boundary

Source: [Master Business Blueprint](../Inbox/health-records-365-business-blueprint.md) §29.

Health Records 365 should initially stay on the **conservative side** of the line between **record management** and **clinical decision-making**.

## In scope

- retrieving information from the customer's own record
- summarising historical events
- showing what a clinician previously recommended
- identifying that no later outcome is visible in the available record
- comparing records over time
- surfacing contradictions
- preparing information for appointments
- preparing provider packs
- explaining what the historical record says
- supporting general provider research or navigation
- helping a customer formulate questions for their clinician

## The wording test

Safe — a statement about the record:

> **"Your consultant recommended a repeat test and no later result is visible in the records we have."**

Materially different — a personalised clinical recommendation:

> **"You should get a CT scan."**

The second should not be casually folded into the record-management product. This test governs [open-item wording](../product/open-loops.md) and every answer from [Ask your medical history](../product/ask-your-history.md).

## If the boundary ever moves

If the business later wants to provide personalised clinical recommendations, this should be designed **deliberately** — potentially with clinician review and a proper assessment of UK medical-device and professional-regulatory obligations based on intended purpose.

The service should not position itself as a replacement for doctors. See [what we are not](../strategy/what-we-are-not.md).

## Note on benchmarking

[Chronicle Health](../market/competitors/chronicle-health.md) maintains the same conservative posture — no diagnosis, prognosis, treatment recommendation or medical advice. This appears to be the category norm rather than a differentiator.
