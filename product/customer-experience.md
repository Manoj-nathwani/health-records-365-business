# Customer experience

Source: [Master Business Blueprint](../Inbox/health-records-365-business-blueprint.md) §11.

The service should feel like having a trusted person or organisation maintain an important part of the customer's life.

## The seven steps

1. **Give us what you have.** The customer does not need to organise everything first.
2. **We help collect the rest.** Where appropriate, Health Records 365 helps the customer obtain relevant records.
3. **We reconstruct the history.** Information is connected across providers and time.
4. **We show uncertainty and gaps.** Open threads, contradictions and missing outcomes become visible.
5. **The customer receives a usable history.** Not merely an archive.
6. **We keep it current.** New healthcare is incorporated as it happens.
7. **We handle what comes next.** When something needs doing — a specialist, a check-up, the test that was never booked — we [plan and arrange the episode](./navigation.md): the facility researched wherever they are, the appointment made, the history prepared, the outcomes brought home.

This is the customer-side view of [the core operating loop](../strategy/operating-loop.md).

## The emotional outcome

The strongest outcomes are **relief** and **control**.

Before:

> "I have years of records scattered everywhere and I don't really know what matters."

After:

> "Someone has gone through it. It's organised. I understand what's outstanding. If a doctor needs something, I know it's there."

And:

> "My healthcare may be spread across several organisations and countries, but my medical history isn't."

**Marketing should generally avoid fear.** The service is about being organised, prepared and in control. See the [language guide](../brand/language.md).
