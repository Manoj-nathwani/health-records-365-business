# The record review

**A consultation with a GP who has read your complete record before you speak.** Delivered through a telemedicine partner, not by us — step 4 of [clinical capability](../strategy/clinical-capability.md), made concrete.

## The use case, from customer zero

The founder's own record generated a precise list of needs, and none of them was diagnosis:

- **A plan for a battery of tests** — which to buy, in what order, what each result would decide
- **Resuming care that died administratively** — a colonoscopy lost to an address change, a recheck agreed and never booked, follow-ups that lapsed when letters went astray
- **Someone to order what the record says is due** — the NHS GP declined three test requests in two weeks; the private route had nobody attached to it
- **A clinical sense-check** of a self-directed plan built from the record

That is not "ask your medical history" and it is not a diagnosis appointment. It is **planning a course of action over a complete record** — a consultation type that barely exists, because no GP anywhere is ever handed a complete record.

And the founder is the *easy* case: he plans his own care to an unusual degree. The median customer stops at *"so what do I do?"* — they need this more, not less.

## What the product is

**Build the record → review it with a GP → leave with a plan → outcomes return to the record.**

1. We deliver the completed history, including [unresolved items](./open-loops.md)
2. The partner GP reads it before the appointment — the preparation is the product
3. A consultation, video or in person: what is outstanding, what is worth testing, what to resume, what to ignore
4. The GP orders tests or refers where clinically appropriate — **under their registration, their judgement, their indemnity**
5. Results and letters come back to us and the [loops close](./open-loops.md) on the record

Step 5 is the point. Today we observe open loops; with a partner who can order, **the lifecycle completes inside the service** — which is what [the differentiation](../market/differentiation.md) has been promising all along.

## Why a tele-GP partner fits, specifically

- **The incentive problem inverts.** An NHS GP is rationing; a private GP is serving. "Patient requests tests with a complete organised record attached" is their easiest and safest consultation of the week
- **Our preparation is their dream input.** GPs make decisions on fragments; a reconciled thirty-year history with the open items flagged makes their clinical judgement better and faster — we make *them* look good
- **Telemedicine matches the customer's geography**, with the cross-border caveat below
- **The regulatory weight sits where it belongs** — with a registered, indemnified clinician

## What to require of the partner

| Requirement | Why |
| --- | --- |
| GMC-registered GPs, CQC-registered service | The registration is the point of the partnership |
| **Will read the record before the appointment**, and price for that time | A GP skimming in the first five minutes destroys the product |
| Can order private tests and imaging, and refer onward | Without ordering, loops still do not close |
| Clear position on **where their patients may be located** | The [cross-border question](../strategy/clinical-capability.md) — many UK tele-GPs serve UK-resident patients only; ask early |
| Results routed back to us by default | The [retention loop](../commercial/retention-loop.md) depends on outcomes returning |
| Accepts our format as the input | The record is the interface; if they demand their own forms, the value evaporates |

## The firewall, stated up front

**We must have no financial interest in what the GP orders.** A flat referral or integration arrangement is fine; a cut of tests ordered is not — it turns the open-items list into a sales funnel and spends [the trust position](../trust/data-sovereignty.md). The record side and the clinical side stay financially separate, and we say so publicly.

## Commercially

- **Sold as an add-on to [prepare it for care](../commercial/layers.md)**, or bundled into the annual tier — it is the strongest possible content for the [premium tier](../commercial/pricing.md)
- The customer pays the GP for the consultation (or we pass it through at cost); **our margin stays on the record work**, which keeps the firewall clean
- For the [adviser channel](../go-to-market/sojourners-capital.md): "a doctor who has actually read your file" is an easier sentence to sell than "a managed record"

## The open questions

1. **Cross-border**: which partners will consult with a patient physically in Indonesia or the UAE, and under what terms? This decides whether the product works for [the core segment](../market/target-customer.md) or only for UK visits home
2. Does the GP charge for reading time, and what does an hour of preparation cost?
3. Will they route results back to a third party as standard?
4. One partner or a panel — and does the founder's telemedicine network shortcut this? **Worth asking before any cold search: who does he already know running exactly this?**

## How to test it

**Customer zero again.** The founder has a test battery planned and dormant care to resume — run his own list through a candidate tele-GP as a paying patient, with the record as the input. One consultation answers most of the table above, reveals what record-prepared GPs actually do with the material, and costs a consultation fee.
